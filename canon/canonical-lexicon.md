# Canonical Lexicon — Tattvashila

**Document Type:** Semantic Stability Reference  
**Canonical Status:** Primary  
**Scope:** All Tattvashila manuscripts, publications, editorial texts, and institutional communications  
**Language Direction:** Hindi-first. Sanskrit-rooted. Devanagari canonical script.

---

## Governing Principle

This lexicon exists to prevent semantic drift.

Philosophical terminology carries meaning that accumulates over centuries of usage
within its tradition. When a term is carelessly translated, paraphrased, or replaced
with a modern English equivalent, the accumulated meaning is lost — and the loss is
often invisible. A casual reader may believe they understand; what they have received
is a different concept under a familiar-sounding label.

This lexicon defines each canonical term's:

- **Devanagari form** — the canonical written form
- **Transliteration** — IAST standard
- **Canonical meaning** — as used within Tattvashila's manuscripts
- **Semantic boundary** — what the term does and does not encompass
- **Prohibited substitutions** — English or simplified equivalents that must not replace the term

The lexicon does not attempt to be exhaustive. It records only terms that are central
to Tattvashila's philosophical framework and at risk of semantic dilution.

## The Terms

---

### तत्त्व — Tattva

**Transliteration:** Tattva  
**Domain:** Philosophical ontology

**Canonical meaning:**  
The essential nature of a thing — not its appearance, classification, or function,
but what it fundamentally is. In the Tattvashila context, Tattva refers to the
ground of inquiry itself: the search for what is actually the case beneath
intellectual superimposition, conventional understanding, and conditioned habit.

Tattva in Tattvashila's usage is not a metaphysical absolute (as in some Shaiva
traditions) but rather the philosophical disposition of asking: what is this really?
What is the actual nature of this experience, this discipline, this relationship,
this life?

**Semantic boundary:**  
Tattva encompasses: essential nature, fundamental inquiry, the question of what is.  
Tattva does not encompass: truth in the English propositional sense, factual
accuracy, abstract principle, or metaphysical essence as an unchanging substance.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "truth" (loses the sense of inquiry and arriving; implies proposition)
- "essence" (implies substance-metaphysics not intended)
- "reality" (loses the active questioning quality)
- "principle" (loses the phenomenological depth)

---

### प्रश्न — Prashna

**Transliteration:** Prashna  
**Domain:** Philosophical method

**Canonical meaning:**  
A genuine question — not a rhetorical device, not a pedagogical prompt, not an
examination question. Prashna in Tattvashila's usage is an inquiry that does not
presuppose its answer. It is a question that the questioner genuinely does not know
how to resolve — and whose unresolved quality is philosophically productive rather
than deficient.

Tattvaprashna as a text is constituted by Prashnas — not as a list of unanswered
questions, but as a sustained inhabitation of foundational uncertainty that
gradually produces philosophical orientation.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Prashna encompasses: genuine inquiry, open questioning, sustained examination.  
Prashna does not encompass: rhetorical questions, examination formats, problem-
statements seeking solutions, or Socratic method in the agonistic Greek sense.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "question" alone (acceptable when used literally, but must not flatten the depth)
- "problem" (implies a solution is sought and findable)
- "challenge" (implies opposition or difficulty to overcome)
- "inquiry" alone (acceptable, but less philosophically precise)

---

### तत्त्वशिला — Tattvashila

**Transliteration:** Tattvashila  
**Domain:** Institutional identity

**Canonical meaning:**  
"The foundation stone" — शिला (shilā) being stone, foundation, bedrock.
Tattvashila names the institution and its orientation: the careful, slow, patient
laying of a philosophical foundation. Not a building. Not a finished structure.
The stone beneath whatever will eventually stand.

Tattvashila as a name communicates that the institution's work is foundational —
not in the sense of being elementary or introductory, but in the architectural
sense of being what must be established before anything else can be placed upon it.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Tattvashila encompasses: foundational philosophical work, institutional permanence,
slow and careful preparation.  
Tattvashila does not encompass: a finished philosophical system, a complete body
of work, a school in the formal sense, or an organization in the modern NGO sense.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
No English equivalent. The name is used as given, in both Hindi and English contexts.

---

### ग्रन्थ — Grantha

**Transliteration:** Grantha  
**Domain:** Manuscript typology

**Canonical meaning:**  
A composed text — specifically, a sustained, systematically composed manuscript
of philosophical or doctrinal significance. In Tattvashila's usage, Grantha
refers to the major texts in the Granthālaya: works of significant length and
institutional weight that are composed over extended periods and placed in the
archive when complete.

Grantha is distinguished from:
- **Lekh** (लेख) — a shorter written piece, essay, or note
- **Tīkā** (टीका) — a commentary on an existing text
- **Sūtra** (सूत्र) — an aphoristic, condensed doctrinal statement

**Semantic boundary:**  
Grantha encompasses: sustained philosophical manuscript, institutionally composed text.  
Grantha does not encompass: blog posts, essays, articles, chapters in isolation,
or any text composed for periodic publication.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "book" (implies commercial publication and consumer reading)
- "volume" (acceptable only when referring to a specific grantha within a series)
- "text" (acceptable as a general reference, but Grantha carries institutional weight)
- "work" (acceptable as a general reference)

---

### ग्रन्थालय — Granthālaya

**Transliteration:** Granthālaya  
**Domain:** Institutional space

**Canonical meaning:**  
The manuscript library of Tattvashila — from Grantha (manuscript) and Ālaya
(आलय, abode, repository, place of dwelling). Not a library in the sense of a
collection open for borrowing, but the institutional space in which the composed
manuscripts reside permanently.

Granthālaya is where manuscripts are placed when they are complete. It is not
a publication platform, a content system, or a reading library. It is an abode —
a place where completed institutional texts dwell.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Granthālaya encompasses: institutional manuscript repository, the permanent
residence of completed granthas.  
Granthālaya does not encompass: content management, publication pipeline,
reading list, blog category, or digital library in the commercial sense.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "library" (acceptable only with the understanding above; loses the composed-text specificity)
- "publications page" (entirely wrong register)
- "reading list" (entirely wrong register)
- "content section" (entirely wrong register)

---

### धर्म — Dharma

**Transliteration:** Dharma  
**Domain:** Philosophical and ethical foundation

**Canonical meaning:**  
In Tattvashila's usage, Dharma is not a religion, a legal code, or a cosmic duty
in the prescriptive sense. It is the living structure of rightness — the quality
of acting in accordance with what one is, what the situation requires, and what
has been cultivated through awareness and discipline. Dharma in this context is
not given from outside; it is understood from within, through the kind of inquiry
that Tattvaprashna initiates.

Tattvashila's institutional declaration — "Grounded in Dharma. Carried with
integrity." — uses Dharma in this living, inquiry-oriented sense.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Dharma encompasses: living rightness, ethical orientation emerging from awareness,
the structural quality of integrity in action.  
Dharma does not encompass: religion (as an institutional affiliation), law (as
external prescription), duty (as mechanical obligation), or cosmic order (as an
impersonal metaphysical law).

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "religion" (categorically wrong in Tattvashila's context)
- "duty" (loses the awareness dimension; implies mechanical compliance)
- "law" (loses the inner dimension)
- "righteousness" (carries moralistic connotation not intended)

---

### साधना — Sādhanā

**Transliteration:** Sādhanā  
**Domain:** Practice philosophy

**Canonical meaning:**  
Sustained, disciplined inner practice — specifically, the kind of practice that
accumulates slowly over time through voluntary commitment rather than external
enforcement. Sādhanā in Tattvashila's usage applies to the inner life of awareness:
the ongoing, patient practice of attention, restraint, and reflection that gradually
produces philosophical orientation.

Sādhanā is not goal-oriented in the outcome-measurement sense. It is a way of
living with awareness that becomes its own form of orientation over time.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Sādhanā encompasses: sustained voluntary inner practice, contemplative discipline,
long-term awareness cultivation.  
Sādhanā does not encompass: habit formation (as a behavioral technique), routine
(as a time-management structure), or self-improvement (as achievement-orientation).

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "practice" (acceptable as a general reference, but Sādhanā carries the sustained
  contemplative quality that "practice" often loses)
- "discipline" (acceptable, but loses the devotional-continuity dimension)
- "habit" (entirely wrong register; implies behavioral automatism)
- "routine" (entirely wrong register)

---

### विवेक — Viveka

**Transliteration:** Viveka  
**Domain:** Philosophical faculty

**Canonical meaning:**  
The faculty of discernment — the capacity to distinguish between what appears
to be and what is; between immediate impulse and considered action; between
the conditioned response and the aware one. Viveka is not merely intellectual
discrimination but a cultivated faculty of the whole person — cognitive, affective,
and volitional — that develops through Sādhanā.

In the taxonomy category Viveka-sādhanā (Awareness & Discipline), the pairing
names the integration: Viveka as the discerning quality; Sādhanā as the sustained
practice through which it is cultivated.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Viveka encompasses: discernment, careful distinction, the cultivated capacity to
see clearly.  
Viveka does not encompass: intelligence (as a cognitive measure), wisdom (as an
accumulated state), or critical thinking (as an academic methodology).

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "wisdom" (loses the active discerning quality; implies an achieved state)
- "intelligence" (loses the philosophical-cultivation dimension)
- "judgment" (implies verdict-giving, not seeing-clearly)
- "critical thinking" (entirely wrong register)

---

### अन्तःकरण — Antaḥkaraṇa

**Transliteration:** Antaḥkaraṇa  
**Domain:** Inner life phenomenology

**Canonical meaning:**  
The inner instrument — the totality of inner faculties: mind (manas), intellect
(buddhi), memory (chitta), and the sense of self (ahaṃkāra) — through which
experience is processed, meaning is made, and intention is formed. Antaḥkaraṇa
is the inner life considered as a structured system, not merely as feeling or
consciousness.

In Tattvashila's taxonomy, the domain "Inner Life" is given its traditional
equivalent as antaḥkaraṇa — the domain of reflections on the quality of inner
experience, attention, fatigue, presence, and the texture of ordinary life.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Antaḥkaraṇa encompasses: the structured inner life, the totality of subjective
faculties, the inner instrument of experience and meaning.  
Antaḥkaraṇa does not encompass: the unconscious (in the Freudian sense), emotions
alone, consciousness in the abstract, or the soul in a religious sense.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "mind" (too narrow; excludes intellect, memory, self-sense)
- "soul" (carries religious connotation not intended)
- "psyche" (carries psychological-clinical connotation)
- "inner world" (too vague; loses the structured quality)

---

### टीका — Tīkā

**Transliteration:** Tīkā  
**Domain:** Manuscript typology — commentary tradition

**Canonical meaning:**  
An exegetical commentary — a text written in sustained engagement with a primary
source, developing philosophical understanding through close reading and careful
elaboration. In Tattvashila's usage, a Tīkā is not a summary, introduction,
or academic commentary in the scholarly apparatus sense. It is an active
philosophical encounter with a text — reading it carefully, in context, with the
specific questions of contemporary ordinary life in view.

A Tīkā begins where a text ends: with what it demands of the reader.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Tīkā encompasses: sustained philosophical engagement with a primary source,
active interpretive encounter, careful elaboration.  
Tīkā does not encompass: summary, annotation, review, academic introduction,
or any text that positions itself above or outside the source text.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "commentary" (acceptable as a general reference, but Tīkā carries the specific
  engaged-reading quality)
- "analysis" (implies external analytical distance)
- "review" (entirely wrong register)
- "notes on" (implies marginalia, not sustained engagement)

---

### संस्था — Saṃsthā

**Transliteration:** Saṃsthā  
**Domain:** Institutional identity

**Canonical meaning:**  
Institution — but in Tattvashila's usage, not an organization in the modern
bureaucratic sense. Saṃsthā names the enduring philosophical body constituted
by its texts, its positions, and its accumulated practice of thinking carefully
over time. Tattvashila as a Saṃsthā is defined primarily by what it composes
and preserves — not by its membership, its funding, or its legal structure.

**Semantic boundary:**  
Saṃsthā encompasses: an enduring philosophical body defined by its work and positions.  
Saṃsthā does not encompass: NGO, organization, company, platform, community,
or startup.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "organization" (implies bureaucratic structure)
- "platform" (implies technology product)
- "community" (implies membership and participation)
- "school" (implies curriculum and formal instruction)

---

### चेतना — Chetanā

**Transliteration:** Chetanā  
**Domain:** Philosophical foundation — awareness

**Canonical meaning:**  
Awareness or consciousness — but specifically the living quality of being present
to experience, not consciousness as a metaphysical substance or neurological
correlate. Chetanā in Tattvashila's usage is the fundamental quality from which
all inner life proceeds: the simple fact of there being awareness of what is
happening, inside and outside.

Tattvaprashna's first Prashna — "Chetanā kā prashna" — asks: What is awareness,
and how does it differ from thought?

**Semantic boundary:**  
Chetanā encompasses: living awareness, the quality of presence, consciousness as
lived experience.  
Chetanā does not encompass: consciousness as a philosophical hard problem,
neural correlates of consciousness, sentience as a biological property, or
mindfulness as a technique.

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "consciousness" (acceptable, but loses the living-quality dimension)
- "awareness" (acceptable as a general reference)
- "mindfulness" (entirely wrong register; implies a technique)
- "attention" (too narrow; Chetanā encompasses more than directed attention)

---

### अनुशासन — Anushāsan

**Transliteration:** Anushāsan  
**Domain:** Practice philosophy

**Canonical meaning:**  
Discipline — specifically, voluntary self-governance arising from awareness and
understanding, not from external enforcement or behavioral conditioning. Anushāsan
in Tattvashila's usage is the capacity to hold a chosen orientation over time —
against distraction, fatigue, and the general pressure of contemporary life.

Tattvaprashna's third Prashna — "Anushāsan kā prashna" — asks: What makes a
discipline hold over time, and what causes it to dissolve?

**Semantic boundary:**  
Anushāsan encompasses: voluntary self-governance, sustained inner commitment,
the capacity to hold an orientation over time.  
Anushāsan does not encompass: external discipline (as rule-following), punishment
(as enforcement), productivity (as output optimization), or willpower (as
psychological force).

**Prohibited substitutions:**  
- "self-discipline" (acceptable but implies behavioral technique)
- "willpower" (entirely wrong register)
- "self-control" (implies suppression, not governance from understanding)
- "productivity" (entirely wrong register)

## Lexicon Governance

### How New Terms Are Added

A term is added to this lexicon when:

1. It appears in a canonical manuscript or institutional text with philosophical weight
2. It is at risk of being misunderstood, simplified, or substituted
3. An institutional review determines that its semantic boundaries need explicit definition

Terms are not added for comprehensiveness. They are added for protection.

### How Existing Definitions Are Revised

A canonical definition may be revised only when:

1. The manuscript itself revises its usage of the term (the manuscript is authoritative)
2. A clear semantic error in the existing definition is identified and documented
3. The institutional review process sanctions the revision

Definitions are not revised for stylistic improvement, readability optimization,
or accessibility simplification.

### Precedence

In any conflict between this lexicon and:

- an AI rendering system: the lexicon takes precedence
- a typographic convention: the lexicon takes precedence
- a translation decision: the lexicon takes precedence
- an editor's preference: the lexicon takes precedence
- the manuscript itself: the manuscript takes precedence

The manuscript is the final authority. This lexicon serves the manuscript.
