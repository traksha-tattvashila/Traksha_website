## What Tattvaprashna Is

Tattvaprashna is not the first volume in a sequence. It is the prerequisite to the
sequence itself. It establishes the philosophical ground — the cleared field — from
which systematic composition can begin.

Its method is not argument but inquiry. It holds open the foundational questions:
What is awareness? What is the self that becomes responsible? What makes a discipline
hold over time? These are not answered here. They are examined until the examination
itself becomes a kind of orientation.

Without Tattvaprashna, a reader may encounter the later granthas as positions. With
it, they encounter them as conclusions — conclusions to a line of questioning they have
already followed from the beginning. This is the distinction between doctrine received
and doctrine understood.

The text is in preparation. It is not being written quickly.

---

## What Tattvaprashna Is Not

Tattvaprashna is not:

- a philosophical introduction in the Western academic sense
- a summary of Tattvashila's positions
- a preparatory reading guide or curriculum framework
- an accessible overview for casual readers
- a motivational opening to a self-improvement sequence

It is a sustained philosophical inquiry — a pregrantha, meaning a text that must
precede all others not because it introduces them but because it prepares the
philosophical capacity required to receive them.

---

## Canonical Institutional Position

Tattvaprashna holds a unique position in the Granthālaya architecture:

- It precedes all granthas in the principal series (Tattvagrantha)
- It precedes all commentaries (Tīkā tradition)
- It precedes all institutional manuscripts (Saṃsthāgata Grantha)
- It is the only pregrantha the institution presently intends to compose

A reader who has passed through Tattvaprashna arrives at all subsequent texts with a
different orientation than one who has not. The institution considers this distinction
philosophically significant — not merely pedagogically convenient.

## Language and Terminology Canon

The manuscript is composed in Hindi. The philosophical terminology is Sanskrit-rooted.

The canonical language hierarchy is:

1. **Hindi prose** — the primary compositional language
2. **Sanskrit terms** — used intact, within Hindi prose, wherever the tradition
   requires them
3. **Devanagari script** — the canonical script for all Hindi and Sanskrit terms
4. **Transliteration** — used only where Devanagari cannot be rendered, following
   the IAST standard
5. **English** — used only where absolutely necessary for institutional communication;
   never used as a replacement for philosophical terminology

No English term may be substituted for a canonical Hindi or Sanskrit philosophical
term without explicit institutional authorization.

## Relationship to the Tattvagrantha Series

Tattvaprashna and the Tattvagrantha series are not the same text. They are related
as preparation is related to systematic elaboration.

| Text | Type | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Tattvaprashna | Pregrantha | Prepares the ground for all subsequent granthas |
| Tattvagrantha | Principal series | Develops the institutional positions |
| Tīkā | Commentary tradition | Active encounters with classical sources |
| Saṃsthāgata Grantha | Institutional manuscripts | Formal positional texts |

The Tattvagrantha series may not be received fully by a reader who has not passed
through Tattvaprashna. The institution acknowledges this and accepts the
consequence: some readers will encounter the later texts first. What Tattvaprashna
provides cannot be substituted by any summary or introduction.
